# Data, assets and dependencies

## DWD weather

Source: **Deutscher Wetterdienst (DWD)**, [DWD open data](https://opendata.dwd.de/weather/), under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), as described in [DWD's reuse terms](https://www.dwd.de/copyright).

- Forecast: `local_forecasts/mos/MOSMIX_L/single_stations/{id}/kml/MOSMIX_L_LATEST_{id}.kmz`.
- Radar: `radar/composite/rv/composite_rv_LATEST.tar` and the observed member of the preceding twelve five-minute archives. As served on 2026-10-22, these contain ODIM HDF5 uint32 ACRR rasters, 1100 × 1200 pixels, with the WGS84 DE1200 polar stereographic projection declared in the files.
- Station metadata: [official MOSMIX station catalogue](https://www.dwd.de/DE/leistungen/met_verfahren_mosmix/mosmix_stationskatalog.cfg?view=nasPublication&nn=16102), bundled as `app/src/main/assets/stations.tsv`. Retrieved 2026-10-22. Catalogue degrees/minutes are converted to decimal degrees. The forecast's own KML coordinates and elevation are displayed with the reading.

The shared app header shows the DWD source note (`Quelle: Deutscher Wetterdienst`) on every screen; the Places screen’s Data & licences dialog carries the full attribution and licence texts. The app converts Kelvin to Celsius and m/s to km/h, groups hourly temperatures into available daily ranges, and totals hourly rain/sunshine only when all intervals of a complete local day exist. Wind in the outlook is the maximum provided hourly speed. Daily condition is the most severe supplied hourly condition. Each day’s start and end are derived independently from its local civil date with `atStartOfDay`, so days whose midnight is skipped or repeated remain correct; completeness requires every hourly source interval from that local start to the next local start, so 23- and 25-hour days can be complete while missing or non-hourly intervals are not totalled. Forecast values remain station forecasts; the app does not spatially interpolate them. Relative humidity is only displayed if DWD provides it, and is never calculated from dew point.

The parser retains `R101` (probability of precipitation greater than 0.1 mm in the preceding hour, already 0–100 percent) and `FX1` (maximum gust in the preceding hour, converted from m/s to km/h). Missing/out-of-range probability and invalid gust values remain unknown; a 3/12-hour gust is never substituted. The shared forecast details card shows current-hour `PPPP` (DWD reduced surface pressure, converted from Pa to hPa) when available, and the selected place’s IANA timezone. Fields and units were checked against DWD’s [element definition](https://opendata.dwd.de/weather/lib/MetElementDefinition.xml) on 2026-10-31. The linked meteoblue page is a visual/quantity reference; the app continues using DWD weather.

Today starts at the selected place’s local midnight and extends through noon on the next local civil date, inclusive. Civil-time boundaries account for DST on either date. Available source timestamps remain unrounded in fractional-offset zones, and absent hours remain unknown. Earlier hours are filled from the preceding DWD MOSMIX L issue covering local midnight, cached separately for offline use. Latest-issue hours take precedence. These are earlier forecasts, not observations. Week totals still require complete individual local days.

Radar pixels use the declared gain, offset, undetect and nodata metadata. The app colours five-minute precipitation amounts, hatches nodata pixels, and projects the raster onto the map. Forecast times come from the HDF5 validity metadata and must agree with the issue and lead in the archive member name. No synthetic frames or extrapolation are used. Missing historical frames leave visible gaps in the timeline and mark the result stale.

The forecast freshness threshold is nine hours from issue; radar is twenty minutes. Connection loss and refresh failures mark retained readings stale immediately. Saved data begins cached while online validation is pending. Issue/validity timestamps remain in the data for freshness calculations. The forecast details card shows the last successful download as “Last updated”; loading saved data or failing a refresh does not advance it.

Optional radar overlays are rendered by DWD’s keyless [WMS 1.3.0 service](https://maps.dwd.de/geoserver/dwd/wms?service=WMS&version=1.3.0&request=GetCapabilities), under DWD's CC BY 4.0 source terms. Temperature uses `Icon-eu_reg00625_fd_gl_T` at 2 m with explicit advertised valid and reference times; the map shows rendered contour colours, not raw interpolated temperatures. Clouds use `Satellite_worldmosaic_3km_world_ir108_3h`, a three-hourly infrared mosaic, with its own UTC valid time; it is context, not synchronized five-minute radar. Satellite imagery derives from EUMETSAT/NOAA sources. Capabilities, available times and server errors change over time; no sample timestamp is a runtime default.

The historical METEOSAT day/night RGB product failed current coverage reads on 2026-11-03. The global infrared mosaic covers northern Germany but has lower contrast and its advertised slots were intermittently unavailable. Wind barbs were omitted after live checks showed thin dark marks, intermittent rendering and no usable zoom-8 sample; they are not represented as a fabricated wind-speed field. At most one supplemental layer is shown beneath precipitation, and selection survives Activity recreation only. Overlay caches use two workers, an 8 MiB bitmap budget and 32 MiB disk budget per layer. Failures or offline buckets leave the basemap and radar usable.

The test resources are genuine DWD samples captured on 2026-10-22: Munich MOSMIX issue 15:00 UTC, observed radar 20:00 UTC, and radar forecast issued at 20:15 UTC and valid at 22:15 UTC. These are test fixtures only and are not packaged as app weather.

### Input validation

A MOSMIX KMZ is accepted only when its inflated content is at most 4 MiB across at most 16 ZIP entries, including entries that are not parsed, and its KML member is at most 1 MiB. The largest checked-in and recorded product (Munich, 2026-10-22) inflates to 344,442 bytes, so these limits retain roughly three times the measured valid size. Parsing stops at 65,536 characters in one text node, 336 time steps and 336 values per forecast element. XML DOCTYPE and entity declarations are rejected; parser features are disabled where the platform supports them, with a byte-level declaration scan as the Android fallback, so internal and external entities are never expanded.

Radar decoding accepts only the complete DE1200 polar-stereographic contract declared by the checked-in ODIM products: `+proj=stere`, `+lat_ts=60`, `+lat_0=90`, `+lon_0=10`, `+x_0=543196.83521776402`, `+y_0=3622588.8619310022`, `+units=m`, WGS84 `+a=6378137` and `+b=6356752.3142451802`, with optional `+no_defs`. Missing, duplicate, unknown or different parameters are rejected with a readable “Unsupported radar projection” error instead of being projected with the DE1200 constants.

## Local sunrise and sunset

Sun times are computed on-device with locally authored NOAA/Meeus-style solar-coordinate mathematics, finding geometric solar-centre crossings at −0.833° within the selected place’s civil day. This is a conventional unobstructed horizon, not a terrain/building/cloud visibility prediction. No astronomy API or device-coordinate request is used at runtime. Polar day/night and skipped civil dates have explicit no-event outcomes. Work is bounded to one worker/latest pending calculation and guarded against obsolete place/date callbacks.

The equations are documented in [NOAA calculation details](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/calcdetails.html). Independent [USNO reference requests](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/data/api) for fixed public-city coordinates were captured on 2026-10-31 for tests (Munich autumn/summer, New York DST day and Sydney summer); tests make no live requests. Reference agreement is tested within two minutes, not claimed as a universal observation guarantee. Real observed times depend on refraction and the local skyline. See `SolarCalculatorTest.kt` for fixed dates/values and [the future sunset plan](future/SUNSET.md) for the separate planned visibility/spot-finder feature.

## City search and optional location

Explicit city searches use the [Open-Meteo geocoding API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/geocoding-api), with [GeoNames](https://www.geonames.org/) place metadata. Query parameters are fixed to `count=10`, `language=de`, `format=json`, with no country filter. Weather still comes exclusively from DWD. Terms checked 2026-10-31: the [free API terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms) allow non-commercial use without an account/key, subject to quotas (600/minute, 5,000/hour, 10,000/day). Commercial use requires a separate provider/licensing decision.

Place metadata is attributed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), following [Open-Meteo's licence](https://open-meteo.com/en/licence). Linked Open-Meteo/GeoNames credit appears beneath the city search field and in Data & licences; selected and edited labels retain provenance in private storage. The checked-in München response is an attributed geocoding test fixture, not runtime weather or a bundled search catalogue.

Time zones from search results, the manual zone prompt and stored places are validated with `java.time.ZoneId`. Legacy aliases that `java.time` cannot use, such as `PST`, are rejected for new entries, skipped when malformed in stored favourites, and fall back to the default place when stored as the selection; compatible valid places remain usable. Favourite rows are parsed independently, so one malformed entry does not hide later valid entries or their provenance. A failed selection or favourite commit restores the previous stored value; a failed favourite deletion leaves the visible list unchanged so the user can retry.

Valid edited queries are submitted after 400 ms without typing; keyboard Search submits immediately and cancels the pending timer. Results use a 20-entry, 30-minute memory cache, cleared by process exit or Clear search cache. After failure, editing or keyboard Search retries; unchanged text never repeatedly retries automatically. Dismissal and backgrounding cancel pending and active work. No search history is written to disk. HTTP redirects stay on the exact approved endpoint, decoded responses are limited to 256 KiB, and Retry-After is respected. Queries, full URLs and device coordinates are not logged.

Use current location requests foreground coarse/fine access together after explicit consent, accepts approximate permission, and obtains one snapshot using eligible Android network/GPS providers. It never requests background access or follows movement. Provider services may use networks; offline acquisition is not guaranteed. Coordinates resolve to stations/time zones on-device, without a reverse-geocoding request. Only the selected snapshot/favourites are stored privately. Weather-station requests and map-tile requests continue to reveal their respective station/viewport to those services.

The bundled MOSMIX catalogue revision is SHA-256 `713df55d858359b021932d84b8dd9c2832b7d73b592e301b2f410c1d7a5b7f3c`. It is validated and loaded once per Repository, then scanned linearly with exact-distance ties broken by station ID. Forecast coverage is 50 km inclusive; points outside it do not trigger a KMZ fetch. A catalogue update re-resolves saved coordinates. The metadata generator validates all fetched outputs before replacing assets; invalid source rows fail with line numbers.

## Basemap

© [OpenStreetMap contributors](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). Data is available under the Open Database License 1.0. Raster tiles are obtained directly from `https://tile.openstreetmap.org/{z}/{x}/{y}.png` and used under the [tile usage policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/).

The map visibly links the attribution to the licence page, identifies the app in its User-Agent and requests tiles only for the displayed viewport. Cached tiles are fresh for seven days, but least-recently-used eviction may remove them sooner to enforce the disk budget. There is no bulk/offline-download feature. Tiles remain runtime cache files and are not redistributed in the repository. The map darkens these tiles locally.

## Local cache limits and integrity

Cache files live in the app's private cache and data directories. Writes use a unique temporary file in the target directory and replace the target atomically, so overlapping workers cannot mix payloads; a failed write leaves the previous file intact. Temporary files abandoned for more than 24 hours are removed at startup and before cache maintenance.

The basemap tile cache is limited to 64 MiB and the MOSMIX forecast/history cache to 32 MiB. Both are pruned at startup and after writes using persisted access recency. Reading a cache entry does not change its file timestamp. Least-recently-used eviction uses the separate access index, while a separate tile receipt record stores each tile's ETag, Last-Modified value and last successful receipt time; that receipt, not the image file's `lastModified`, defines the seven-day freshness window. A stale cached tile is revalidated with `If-None-Match` and `If-Modified-Since` from that record, subject to the existing HTTPS host allow-list, redirect limit and response-size limit. HTTP 304 keeps the cached image and refreshes the receipt; a missing or undecodable cached image is recovered with an unconditional download instead. Network, decode or cache-write failures keep the previous tile and receipt, and freshness is published only after a successful 200 or 304. Pruning removes disk files only; decoded tiles, radar bitmaps and the retained forecast reading in memory are left untouched. Historical `MOSMIX_L_*` files older than three days are removed, and successful writes still replace the previous forecast/history entry. At most 256 failed tile keys are remembered, in access order; the least recently attempted failed key is forgotten first.

Tile validator records whose image has been evicted or removed are pruned when the tile fetcher starts and after cache writes.

Radar frames are cached with a manifest carrying cache and palette versions. On load, the manifest and every referenced PNG are validated (PNG signature, chunk CRCs, complete image data, declared dimensions matching the radar grid). An incompatible version/palette or corrupt/mismatched frame discards the manifest and removes the affected images; the next online refresh regenerates them. Existing valid PNGs are reused only after the same validation. Valid cached frames keep their receipt timestamps for freshness calculations.

Overlay tiles are stored per layer within a 32 MiB budget and reused only while younger than six hours (clouds) or two hours (temperature). Older images are refetched when online; while offline the last usable set is shown as `Cached overlay`. Corrupt or non-256 px images are discarded before reuse.

## Offline time-zone metadata

The files `timezones.tree` and `timezones.txt` are extracted without changing their lookup content from [PhotoStructure tz-lookup](https://github.com/photostructure/tz-lookup) revision [`6051e7e2fe8b754e23d40aff0120cf9b38bde608`](https://github.com/photostructure/tz-lookup/commit/6051e7e2fe8b754e23d40aff0120cf9b38bde608), which identifies package version 11.7.0. Its code and the port in `ZoneLookup.kt` are under [CC0](licenses/tz-lookup-CC0.txt), originally by The Dark Sky Company, LLC, with subsequent PhotoStructure changes. Data retrieved 2026-10-22.

The geographical source is [timezone-boundary-builder](https://github.com/evansiroky/timezone-boundary-builder), © OpenStreetMap contributors, under [ODbL 1.0](licenses/timezone-data-ODbL-1.0.txt). The exact bundled `timezones.tree` and `timezones.txt` remain available under ODbL in the public source mirror at [app/src/main/assets](https://github.com/psyke089/drizziq/tree/main/app/src/main/assets); the private Codeberg development repository is not relied on for this source offer. The generated lookup files keep the upstream payload without an inserted provenance header, so their licence and provenance are recorded here, in the bundled notices and in `docs/licenses/`. Zone lookup is approximate around borders; an unresolved zone opens a focused IANA-zone prompt. Android's installed time-zone rules supply the current civil-time offset.

Run `python3 tools/update_metadata.py` (Python 3.11+, standard library only) to regenerate the station catalogue and pinned timezone data. This needs a network connection; the app uses the bundled metadata offline.

The application uses Android's native UI, HTTPS networking, bitmap renderer and touch APIs. Editable icon sources live in [`artwork/icons/`](../artwork/icons/README.md).
